# Reproduction platforms: landscape and gap check

**Gap statement:** No existing reproduction platform runs systematically, at scale, across Wiley's ecology, life-science, chemistry and materials journals. The gap is not total. CODECHECK (1 Stat Med paper), ReScience C (at least 5 Wiley originals, including one in *Global Change Biology*) and the 2026 AI-agent benchmark SEER-Repro (4 of 20 papers from Wiley's *Cancer* and *Cancer Medicine*) have each reproduced a few Wiley papers. Two Wiley-published social-science journals already get systematic checks: every quantitative *AJPS* paper is verified by Odum, and *Econometrica* runs Data Editor checks.

> Pitch implication: say "no platform covers Wiley's **ecology, life-science, chemistry and materials** portfolio systematically". Do not say "existing platforms don't cover Wiley journals". That broader claim is false for health/medical statistics (a few papers), economics and political science (systematic coverage).

Research date: 2026-10-20. Time-boxed, about 45 minutes. Wiley checks used DOI prefixes 10.1002 and 10.1111 and the journal names.

## Report formats worth borrowing

- **CODECHECK manifest plus certificate DOI:** a machine-readable list of every regenerated output (one row per figure or table, with a comment), packaged as a citable certificate with a DOI. This fits Wiley well because it can link back from the article page.
- **SSRP claim-level 0–10 scale:** score each claim or display item separately rather than giving one pass/fail. "Fully reproducible from raw data" is level 10, and the lower levels say what is missing (data, code, or both).
- **SEER-Repro atomic-claim verdicts plus a robustness pass:** split the paper into atomic numeric claims, label each reproduced / close / not reproduced, and give a failure category (data unavailable, underspecified method, record revisions). Then flag analytical choices that could flip a conclusion. This is the closest template to our product.
- **I4R standardized protocol plus author response:** fixed sections (data/code availability, computational reproducibility, coding errors, preregistration deviations, robustness), with the original authors' reply attached. This builds trust and reduces publisher risk.
- **AJPS / Econometric Society badges:** a simple, visible outcome (badge or "verified" stamp) on the article, backed by the detailed report. This is already familiar to Wiley's own AJPS and Econometrica editors.
